The Product

Two modes, one hiking habit.

The individual mode is the engagement engine and the data source. The fantasy layer is the business — the part nobody else has built.

🧭
Mode 01

Individual Quest Mode

A gamified personal hiking tracker that turns real trails into curated challenges.

  • Quests tied to real trails — summit before sunset, hit 10K ft of elevation this season
  • Import from Strava, Apple Health, or Google Fit — no GPS build required for MVP
  • XP, levels, streaks, and a badge case on your profile
  • Geofenced checkpoints verify you actually visited the trail, not just opened the app
🏆
Mode 02 — The Differentiator

Fantasy League Mode

Real hikers become draftable players. Their verified stats generate fantasy points for the managers who own them.

  • Draft night: snake-draft real hikers from your school onto your roster
  • Open scoring formula — points per mile, per 1,000 ft elevation, per verified quest
  • Head-to-head weekly matchups, standings, waivers, trades, and playoffs
  • School-vs-school leagues — the PlayVS playbook, applied to the outdoors
Why Now

Every piece of this exists. Nobody has combined them.

A quick scan of the adjacent categories — tracking apps, fantasy-for-real-life apps, and school league infrastructure — shows a clear, unclaimed gap.

Tracking

Strava & AllTrails

Best-in-class individual and social tracking. No draft mechanics, no team-vs-team layer.

RPG Skin

Fantasy Hike

Turns steps into an avatar quest — near-zero adoption, and you can't draft real people.

Closest Match

Life League

Fantasy-format drafting of habits, not real people — validated concept, ~50+ downloads. Wide open.

League Infra

IMLeagues at Cal Poly

Proves campus rec departments already run drafts, rosters, and standings — just not for hiking.

The Playbook

PlayVS

Built interscholastic esports leagues from nothing by partnering directly with schools. Same move, new sport.

Beachhead

Cal Poly / CPVC

Existing rec-sports muscle, an outdoor-rec program, and a built-in student player base to pilot with.

The whitespace: nobody has combined verified real-world activity data, fantasy-draft mechanics over real peers, and formal school-vs-school league structure. That's the bet.
Roadmap

Ship the cheapest version first.

The riskiest assumption isn't the GPS tracking — it's whether people will actually draft their classmates and check standings weekly. Test that before building an app.

Phase 0 — Validate manually

1–2 weeks

20–40 people, a shared spreadsheet, and hand-scored Strava data. If people won't draft friends and check standings by spreadsheet, an app won't fix that.

Phase 1 — MVP web app

4–8 weeks

Next.js + Supabase. Strava/Health import, draft interface, automated scoring, standings, and one free pilot league to gather real usage data.

Phase 2 — Native app + quests

2–4 months

In-house GPS tracking, geofenced quest checkpoints, chat, waivers, trades — and a second pilot league to test the multi-tenant model.

Phase 3 — Inter-school expansion

Ongoing

School-vs-school leagues, a self-serve admin dashboard for rec departments, and eventually other trackable activities beyond hiking.

Business Model

Free to prove it. Paid once people want it back.

No monetization in Phase 0–1 — engagement gets proven at one campus first.

01

B2C Freemium

Quest mode free forever; a paid tier unlocks premium quests, deeper stats, and team cosmetics.

02

League Entry Fees

A small per-season buy-in per manager — the same model campus intramural registration already uses.

03

B2B School Licensing

License the admin layer to rec departments and, eventually, high school athletic associations.

04

Sponsorship

Outdoor gear brands sponsoring quests or leagues, plus affiliate links on completed-quest gear.
