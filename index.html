The gap in the market

Every other sport has a school league. Hiking doesn't.

Football, basketball, even esports have formal school-vs-school infrastructure. Hiking has trackers and step-counting RPGs — but nothing lets you draft real people the way fantasy football lets you draft real quarterbacks.

Individual only

Strava

Clubs, segments, and challenges drive retention — but it's social, not a draft-and-compete league structure.

Individual only

AllTrails

Trail discovery plus a new AI-planning tier. Still a solo tool — no team or league layer at all.

Low adoption

Fantasy Hike

An RPG skin on step-counting. Near-zero ratings. You move an avatar — you don't draft other real people.

Closest, but tiny

Life League

Drafts habits, not people — friends score points on activities. Validates the format, barely exists (50+ installs).

The product

Two modes fused into one app

The individual mode is the engagement engine and the data source. The fantasy layer is the business — the part nobody else has built.

🥾

Individual Quest Mode

A gamified personal hiking tracker that generates the verified stat lines the fantasy layer runs on.

  • Curated quests on real trails — "summit before sunset," "5 trails this month"
  • Import from Strava, Apple Health, and Google Fit for MVP speed
  • Geofenced checkpoints so a "completion" means you were physically there
  • XP, levels, streaks, badge case, personal profile stats
🏆

Fantasy League Mode

Real hikers become draftable players. Real stats become fantasy points. This is the differentiator.

  • Snake draft real people from your school or town league onto a roster
  • Head-to-head matchups, standings, playoffs, and a championship
  • Waiver wire, trades, team name/logo, group chat, weekly recaps
  • Inter-school leagues — your campus's roster vs. theirs

League structure

A season that feels like real sports

Draft night, a regular season, playoffs, a championship — the same rhythm fantasy football and PlayVS's interscholastic esports leagues already use.

1

Preseason

Roster lock and draft night. Managers snake-draft real hikers from their own campus or town league.

2

Regular Season

Weekly head-to-head matchups scored from verified stats. Waiver wire and trades stay open.

3

Playoffs

Top teams bracket off. Inter-school leagues surface the best campus rosters against each other.

4

Championship

A single champion team per league, per season — fall and spring seasons mirror the school year.

Admin / School Layer

A rec-department dashboard to create leagues and seasons, verify eligibility, manage trailhead partnerships and waivers, and moderate disputes — the layer that turns this from a consumer app into something a campus rec department can officially license.

Anti-Cheating & Data Integrity

GPS speed-anomaly detection, geofenced checkpoint verification, a Verified vs. Self-Reported flag on every stat line, and a manager-facing dispute flow reviewed by admins — because real stats now carry competitive stakes.

Build plan

Cheapest possible test first

The risky assumption isn't the GPS tracking — it's whether people will actually draft their classmates and check standings weekly. That gets tested before a line of production code.

Phase 0 1–2 weeks

Manual pilot with 20–40 people on a shared spreadsheet, scored by hand from Strava data. Confirm people draft their friends, check standings unprompted, and talk trash about it. In parallel, reach out to Cal Poly ASI Rec Sports.

Phase 1 4–8 weeks

MVP web app: auth, Strava/Health/Fit import, league creation, snake draft, automated scoring, standings, team pages. Launch with one free pilot league.

Phase 2 2–4 months

Native app with in-house GPS tracking and geofenced quests, push notifications, chat, waiver wire and trades. Add a second pilot — a second school or a town league.

Phase 3 Ongoing

School-vs-school leagues, a self-serve admin dashboard for rec departments, and expansion beyond hiking into running and biking on the same fantasy engine.

Built by

DaBigR — Cal Poly, CPVC

A Cal Poly student building Hiking Quest from an unfair advantage most founders don't have: Cal Poly's ASI Rec Sports already runs IMLeagues for intramural sports, proving campus rec departments have the operational muscle and player base for exactly this kind of league — it just hasn't been built for outdoor rec yet. The plan follows PlayVS's playbook: build the league infrastructure yourself, prove it at one campus, then expand school by school.
